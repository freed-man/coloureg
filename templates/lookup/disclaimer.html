Where the data comes from

coloureg combines data from several third-party providers, including official UK vehicle data services and paint colour databases. We don't generate paint codes ourselves — we pass through what these sources return, with some processing to give you the best available result.

No guarantee of accuracy

The third-party data we rely on can be incomplete, outdated, or wrong. Common reasons:

  • Manufacturer databases sometimes return abbreviated codes rather than the full factory code. We try to expand abbreviations where we can.
  • Your car may have been resprayed, in which case the factory code no longer applies.
  • Some paint codes are reused by manufacturers across years, models, and trims — meaning the same code can refer to different colours.
  • Vinyl wraps don't change the underlying paint, but if your car is wrapped, the visible colour won't match the factory code.

Always verify the code against your vehicle's paint code label — typically found on a sticker in the driver's door jamb, under the bonnet, or in the boot. The label is the authoritative source for your specific car.
